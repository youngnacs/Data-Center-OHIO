# 07 — Civil, structure, and physical security

## Civil

- Geotech campaign in P0: bearing, liquefaction, karst if applicable, mine maps (eastern Ohio), floodplain.
- Pads above the 500-year flood and above any riverine brownfield surprise. Muskingum / Ohio River sites live or die here.
- Stormwater: Ohio EPA construction general permit, extended detention, no cooling-chemical story in the outfall because we are not in the evaporative business.
- Wetlands: isolate, mitigate, do not pretend a 1,100-acre Ohio site has none.
- Roads: transformer turning radii, construction village, and a permanent loop that fire apparatus can run at 3 a.m. in ice.

## Structure

- Ohio snow, ice, and wind. Drift at dry-cooler screens. Ice falling from coolers is an ops hazard — design walkways.
- Tornado / severe wind: envelope that keeps the white space closed; generator and BESS enclosures rated accordingly. This is not a coastal hurricane building and not an unreinforced warehouse.
- Type B vibration and floor flatness for liquid manifolds and high-mass racks.
- Fire: NFPA 75/76, battery rooms (NFPA 855), liquid coolant SDS and containment, detection that works in a 100 kW/rack aisle.

## Physical security

- Concentric rings: public berm, vehicle control, fence, building, hall, cage.
- Two-person integrity on Type C, MMRs, and 345 kV control houses.
- Standoff from the public road; no parking against Type B skins.
- Campus lighting and cameras designed for Ohio winter fog and snow, not a desert data-sheet.
- Construction-phase security is when most copper and tools vanish — budget it as a system, not a guard shack.

## Resilience domains

Do not stack BESS, diesel, glycol storage, and the NOC in one blast/fire zone. The site diagram in [03](03-campus-master-plan.md) exists to force that conversation before a civil engineer “optimizes” the yard.
