# 09 — Sustainability and community

License to operate in Ohio in this decade is **earned in public**.

## Carbon and energy

- **Annual 100% CFE match by 2032** via PJM-region wind/solar PPAs + storage. Ohio and adjacent PJM zones only; do not “match” with a far-away RECs story the legislature will mock.
- **24/7 CFE on Type C and Type A Gold by 2038.** Type B will lag until SMR/H2/long-duration storage exists; be honest about that in filings.
- BTM gas in P2 is a **reliability bridge**. Permit it as such, with H2-ready equipment and a written sunset or blend-down tied to P3 technology gates.
- Publish PUE and CFE matching yearly. Hide-nothing is cheaper than a hearing.

## Water

Closed-loop is the sustainability design and the political design. Report:

- Makeup gallons
- Adiabatic-assist hours
- WUE
- Source (never “the city” if it is reuse or well)

Offer a public dashboard. The draft Ohio bills already want ODNR/OEPA reporting; beating them to it is cheaper than fighting.

## Ecology

- Habitat acres are not leftover dirt. They are the berm that makes the township meeting survivable.
- Dark-sky lighting, noise limits on dry coolers and BTM, and a construction-hours compact.
- If brownfield: remediation is a community deliverable, not only a liability screen.

## Tax and local economics

Do not underwrite the IRR on a **100% sales-tax exemption**. Underwrite on:

- PILOT that a county can defend
- Property-tax path that survives a 50% abatement cap
- Jobs that are real: construction peak 2,500–4,000; ops 350–450; plus a supply chain in Ohio electrical and mechanical
- Apprenticeship seats written into EPC contracts

If brownfield + BTM lands a **75% exemption**, that is upside.

## Transparency

No NDAs that hide water use or tax abatements. Those NDAs are already a legislative target. Heartland’s community compact says the opposite: **we will publish the numbers this architecture already meters.**
